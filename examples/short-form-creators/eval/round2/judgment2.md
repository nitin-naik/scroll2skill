# Judgment 2

## Task 1: SIP script (X vs Y)

**X has, Y lacks**
1. A hook that is relatable and works with the sound off: a portfolio screenshot showing "2 years of SIP. ₹6,000 profit?!" mirrors the viewer's own app. Y opens with "This one SIP mistake can cost ₹10 lakh", then says "Three mistakes", which contradicts its own hook and reads as clickbait.
2. A CTA the viewer can act on and share: "Open your app tonight. If it says 'Regular', fix that first. Send this to the friend who paused..." Y's ending is the generic "Ask your SIP doubt in comments."
3. A tighter script with no filler: Y spends about 4 of its 40 s on bridge lines ("Common one. The next is sneakier.", "Now the expensive one."). X also shows the units-per-NAV maths on screen, which teaches the point visually.

**Y has, X lacks**
1. The "check exit load and tax before switching" warning is spoken inside the reel itself. X puts it only in the caption and the comment-reply tip, so viewers who only watch could switch blindly.
2. The standard mutual fund disclaimer appears on screen as an end card, and there are SEBI finfluencer compliance notes (2024 rules, stay educational unless registered as an RIA or RA).
3. A full Hinglish version of the script, plus a more conservative ₹10 lakh figure (12% vs 11% compounded annually, giving ₹92L vs ₹81.6L), with the method explained.

**Factual issues**
- X: "SEBI's riskometer ... suggest a horizon of 5 years" is wrong, because the riskometer rates risk level, not holding period. Its ₹12–13L gap is right under the monthly-rate (rate ÷ 12) convention that most calculators use (₹99.9L vs ₹87.4L). Both are minor.
- Y: no errors. It makes no claim about a fixed expense-ratio gap and points readers to AMFI or Value Research to verify it. It is padded with meta sections ("What I added myself", "I couldn't confirm ... in 2026").

**Winner: X.** It has the stronger hook and CTA in a tighter script you could film today. Y is the more cautious on compliance but is weaker as a reel and padded with meta commentary.

## Task 2: CapCut edit plan (X vs Y)

**X has, Y lacks**
1. A fix for each platform's CTA: YouTube's word is "subscribe", not "follow" (re-record the line or change the on-screen text), and Shorts' "Related video" link can point to Part 2. Y posts the same edit, with "follow", on both platforms.
2. A tighter runtime of 33–37 s. It drops the "here's the thing nobody tells you" bridge and offers an optional loop ending for Shorts.
3. A real proof beat in the first frame (the Studio screenshot at "fifty thousand", about 0:03), and the same frame reused as the Reels cover with the 3:4 grid crop in mind.

**Y has, X lacks**
1. Caption text for every line, split into 1–4 word chunks with the emphasis words marked. You could type the captions straight in.
2. Click-by-click CapCut steps for each technique (snap zoom to 135% with no keyframe, rounded-corner masked picture-in-picture, a 4-segment progress bar, the full-screen interrupt card, turning off the default ending).
3. A b-roll shot list that says where each shot comes from (screen-record, stock, or film it yourself), plus two planned pattern interrupts at set times.

**Factual issues**
- Neither has a clear error. Y honestly tags its uncertain menu paths as "(inferred)".
- X's "10–15% ≈ −25 to −30 dB" mapping is rough but harmless.
- Y runs about 40 s, longer than it needs to be. It is also wordier and more dogmatic ("exactly 2 interrupts... Don't add a third").

**Winner: X (narrowly).** It has equal usefulness, tighter focus, and the "subscribe" vs "follow" fix for Shorts that Y misses. Y is more step-by-step, but longer than it needs to be.
