# Reel script: "Why your SIP isn't making money: 3 mistakes"

**Payoff (what the viewer walks away with):** they can spot the three habits that quietly shrink SIP returns (stopping in a fall, judging too early, paying commission through a regular plan) and know what to check in their own app.

**Frame:** number-first. I don't know your own SIP story, so I didn't make one up. The hook runs on a worked example, clearly labelled "example". If you really have made one of these mistakes, swap the lead for "I made mistake #2 for two years" (in your own words). A true personal line beats the number.

**Length:** about 82 spoken words, roughly 33 to 35 s at a normal pace, with room to breathe inside a 40 s reel.

## 4. Filming notes (phone, solo, at home)

- **Framing:** vertical 9:16, phone at eye level on a stack of books or a tripod, head and shoulders, with space above your head for the text.
- **Light:** face a window. No bright window behind you, and turn off the overhead tube light if it casts shadows.
- **Audio:** sit in a small room with soft things around (bed, curtains). Hold the phone within an arm's length, or plug in wired earphones with a mic and keep the mic near your chest.
- **Delivery:** say the hook with energy, as if telling a friend something surprising. Don't rush. If it runs long, cut words, don't talk faster.
- **Takes:** 3 takes of the hook and 2 of every other line. Film each line as its own clip so you can cut out the gaps between them.
- **Edit:** cut every pause. Open on the hook, with no logo and no "hi guys". Burn in captions, because many people watch with the sound off.

## 6. Check before filming

**Numbers (I checked the maths, but please re-check them):**
- **₹10 lakh gap:** ₹10,000 a month for 20 years (₹24 lakh invested) grows to about **₹92.0 lakh at 12% a year** vs about **₹81.6 lakh at 11%**, a gap of about ₹10.4 lakh (12% and 11% are annual rates, compounded monthly, with each SIP paid at the start of the month). Many online SIP calculators show ~₹12.6 lakh because they just divide the annual rate by 12. I used the lower figure so nobody can say you inflated it. 12% and 11% are **assumptions, not expected returns**, and the on-screen footnote has to say so.
- **"About 1% a year" difference between regular and direct plans:** the real gap varies by fund (often ~0.5–1.5% in equity funds). Check it on a couple of funds on AMFI or Value Research before you say "about 1%". If it's lower for typical funds, say "up to 1%" *(inferred, own knowledge)*.
- **"Equity needs five-plus years":** this is the usual guidance, not a guarantee *(inferred)*.

**Regulatory (my own knowledge, verify current rules):**
- The standard mutual fund disclaimer must be on screen and in the caption *(own knowledge, verify current rules)*.
- SEBI rules for finance creators: unless you are a SEBI-registered investment adviser (RIA) or research analyst (RA), stay educational. Don't name or recommend specific funds and don't promise returns. The 2024 SEBI rules also limit regulated entities (AMCs, brokers) from working with unregistered finfluencers, so if any of this is sponsored, check before posting *(own knowledge, verify current rules)*.
- **Switching from regular to direct is a sale.** It can trigger exit load and short- or long-term capital gains tax. That's why the script says "check exit load and tax first" right after mistake three. Don't cut that line *(own knowledge)*.

**Assumptions about you:**
- I wrote the comment CTA ("I reply to every one") because I assumed you don't have DM automation set up. Only use it if you'll actually reply for the first day or two. If you do have ManyChat (or something similar) running, you could use "Comment SIP and I'll DM you a checklist" instead, but only if the checklist exists and the automation works. Test it first, because I couldn't confirm how reliably it works in 2026.
- I assumed a solo phone shoot at home, mostly English with a Hinglish option. Pick the version your comments are usually written in.
- I didn't add any personal story. If you have a real one, use it in the lead (see "Frame" above).
