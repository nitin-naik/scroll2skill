# Judgment

## Task 1: SIP reel script (X vs Y)

**What X has that Y lacks**
1. Filming instructions for a phone shoot today: face the window, 1080p/30fps, wired mic, one take per row, about 10% more energy. Y has no production guidance.
2. A warning that switching from Regular to Direct is a redemption that can trigger capital gains tax and exit load. This matters because X's own spoken CTA tells viewers to "fix that first".
3. A ready-to-paste caption with the SEBI-style disclaimer, a Hinglish version of the hook (a good fit for an Indian audience), and a worked units example for Mistake 1 (₹50 NAV gives 200 units, ₹40 NAV gives 250).

**What Y has that X lacks**
1. Built-in retention: bridges at 13–16 s ("the second one hurts more") and 21–24 s ("the third one hides in your statement"), and the ₹12 lakh mistake saved for last. X puts its biggest number second.
2. Stronger compliance and honesty guidance: check SEBI's finfluencer rules, don't present a mock-up screen as your own results, and use a personal story only if it is true.
3. Instructions to check the "~1%" fee claim against real funds' expense ratios on AMFI or factsheets, and a comment-reply CTA suited to an 8k account.

**Factual issues**
- Both: the ₹12 lakh figure is correct as an illustration (₹10k/month for 20 years: about ₹99.9L at 12% vs about ₹87.4L at 11%), and both label it as assumed returns.
- X: the spoken line "If it says 'Regular', fix that first" pushes viewers to act without the tax/exit-load caveat, which appears only in the caption notes. The backup hook "Mistake #1 costs the most" contradicts X's own numbers, where Mistake 2 is the ₹12L one. "You lost money twice" is loose.
- Y: "₹12 lakh+ lost" overstates it slightly, since it is lower gains rather than a loss. The hook "SIP flat after two years? Three mistakes cause that" implies a flat SIP is always the investor's fault, when it can simply be the market. The CTA note cites "one creator's video", which can't be checked.

**Winner: X, narrowly.** It can be filmed and posted today with fewer gaps (filming setup, caption, Hinglish, switching caveat), although Y's beat order holds attention better.

## Task 2: CapCut edit plan (X vs Y)

**What X has that Y lacks**
1. Exact caption text for every chunk, with the emphasis words and timings to a tenth of a second for each line.
2. Numbered CapCut steps: splitting to reorder, Scale keyframes for the punch-in, Overlay for b-roll, auto captions and style settings.
3. A deliberate two-interrupt plan (a punch-in plus a b-roll moment where the music drops), with the reason behind each.

**What Y has that X lacks**
1. Much more b-roll, which the creator asked for: a cinema camera crossed out, a feed-scroll screen recording, a meta freeze-frame of the opening, a mute icon, the window shot. X has a single 2 s b-roll.
2. Audio cleanup (Enhance voice / Reduce noise, matched loudness), removing CapCut's auto-added ending clip and watermark, and the Instagram "Upload at highest quality" setting.
3. Platform packaging and checks: Reels caption, Shorts title, Related-video link, a loop option for the Shorts version, a 3:4 grid-crop check on the cover, and a final checklist that includes watching once on mute.

**Factual issues**
- Neither has a significant error. X tags its unsure CapCut menu names as "(inferred)", which is honest. Y's settings (music at 10–15%, Related video, highest-quality upload, CapCut end clip) are accurate.
- Y's b-roll plan needs some stock footage and extra shooting. That is extra work but not wrong.

**Winner: Y.** It covers everything the creator asked for, b-roll especially, and adds audio cleanup and the CapCut end-card fix. X is more precise frame by frame but gives the video almost no visual variety.
