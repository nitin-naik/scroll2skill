# Short-form edit plan

**This skill adds to your own knowledge; it doesn't limit it.** Give the user a complete, timed plan they can follow in their app today. The rules below are what the studied creators agree on; use your own knowledge where they're silent (e.g. app menus, platform safe zones) and mark it *(inferred)*.

## Steps

1. **Decide the cut list first.** From the transcript, mark every line to keep, trim or delete:
   - Delete false starts, fillers ("um", "so yeah") and repeated lines; keep only the best take of each line ([ryancollinsvideo, t=0:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MYdKBuA3XF8&t=8)).
   - Remove gaps between sentences (jump cuts); this applies to both pace styles in step 5: "one-take" means no camera or scene changes, not that silences stay in ([ryancollinsvideo, t=0:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MYdKBuA3XF8&t=11)). Shorten pauses of about 1 s or more ([Descript](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rI3H6tJlD00), on-screen setting; supporting only).
   - Give the new running time.
2. **Fix the first frame and first 3 seconds.** The first frame must already show the payoff, a number or a person mid-gesture; if the take starts slowly, move the strongest line or the result shot to 0:00 (payoff or proof first: 3 of 6 creators studied; see short-form-script). Put 2–4 words of hook text on screen by 0:00.5.
3. **Captions (4 of 6 creators use the same style).** ([ryancollinsvideo, t=0:13–0:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MYdKBuA3XF8&t=13); demonstrated by [vidIQ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oy0jQZHv2hk), [Sky Tan](https://www.instagram.com/p/DambEp1igDW/) and [THE EDITOR GUY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RMBzR2DnEss))
   - 1–4 words per caption, synced to speech, never full sentences.
   - Bold sans-serif, white, all caps, dark shadow or outline.
   - 1 emphasis word per line in a second colour (yellow is the common choice) and ~120% size: numbers and the key noun. In the plan, mark it as `[yellow]word`.
   - Placement: centre or slightly below, clear of the bottom ~20% and the right edge where platform buttons and captions sit *(inferred: platform UI, not from the sources)*.
   - Generate with the app's auto-captions (CapCut: Text → Auto captions; Premiere: Transcribe), then fix every misheard word. Auto-captions mishear names and jargon (one studied video's own auto-captions turned "Hormozi" into "homogeneous").
4. **Pattern interrupts: build a rhythm first, then break it rarely** ([THE EDITOR GUY, t=0:08–1:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RMBzR2DnEss&t=8)).
   - Rhythm = consistent cut pace, steady music and a consistent caption style. An interrupt only registers against a rhythm.
   - Interrupts: a zoom punch-in, a sound or music drop, a b-roll cutaway, a full-screen text card, a colour flash.
   - What counts: a deliberately jarring break (snap punch-in to ~130%+, a sudden music drop, a full-screen text card, a style change). Regular b-roll that illustrates what's being said, small scale changes (100% ↔ ~108%) that hide jump cuts, and picture-in-picture proof overlays are all **rhythm, not interrupts**, so they don't count against the limit *(inferred)*.
   - Dose for a 30–60 s Short or Reel: **2 at most** ("Don't do it more than twice in any short-form video"). The same creator also says "3–4 max" for a "retention edit"; treat 3–4 as the ceiling only for longer, fast-paced edits. Place them where attention would dip: at the first natural pause after the hook (usually 3–8 s) and around the halfway point.
5. **Choose a pace.** Two styles held attention in the studied videos; pick one and keep it consistent:
   - **Cut-driven:** a new shot, zoom or text every 2–3 s (vidIQ ran 3.5 cuts per 10 s; THE EDITOR GUY 5.1).
   - **One-take with overlays:** no cuts at all, with something new on screen every 1–2 s (section labels, a progress bar, gestures). Sky Tan's 35 s reel and SpactYT's Short both had zero cuts. Best for talking-to-camera explainers with a list structure.
6. **B-roll and visual variety.** Something should change on screen every 2–5 s. Plan b-roll for every concrete noun or claim (the phone, the analytics screen, the window light), say for each shot whether the creator films it, screen-records it or uses stock, and keep each 1–3 s *(inferred)*.
7. **Show structure.** For list-style reels ("3 mistakes", "4 tips"), add a small section label ("TIP 2/4") or a segmented progress bar near the top, below the platform's top ~12% *(inferred)*, that fills as you go; it tells viewers there's more coming ([Sky Tan, t=0:04](https://www.instagram.com/p/DambEp1igDW/)).
8. **Sound.** Clean the voice first (CapCut: Enhance voice / Reduce noise; match loudness) *(inferred)*. Music at low volume under speech; a short whoosh or pop on each interrupt and on the CTA ([ryancollinsvideo, t=0:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MYdKBuA3XF8&t=28)). For posting to several platforms, export with voice and sound effects only and add music inside each app from its licensed library, which avoids copyright muting *(inferred)*.
9. **End on the CTA, then cut.** Remove the app's auto-added end clip or watermark *(inferred)*. No outro, no fade. Hold the final text card long enough to read (≈1.5 s) *(inferred)*.

## Output

1. **Cut list**: `Original time | Line | Keep / trim / delete | Why` and the new running time.
2. **Edit table**: `New time | Spoken | Caption (emphasis as [yellow]word) | On screen (b-roll, labels, zoom) | Sound`, as contiguous time ranges (one row per spoken line) from 0:00 to the end. Times after trimming are estimates; say so.
3. **First frame**: exactly what's on screen at 0:00.
4. **App steps**: the specific menus in the user's app for captions, cuts, zooms and text styles, if they named one.
5. **Export and posting**: resolution and frame rate, upload-quality settings, cover frame, and per-platform differences (Reels caption, Shorts title, music added in-app).
6. **Final checklist**: watch once on mute, once at full speed on a phone, check captions for misheard words *(inferred)*.

## Scope and limits

- Sources are 6 short videos; caption styling and pattern interrupts are well supported, while the "+40% retention" claim in THE EDITOR GUY's title is never backed up and is not used.
- App menus change; Premiere's caption UI has changed since the 2022 source. Check menu names in the user's app version.
- Nothing here covers colour grading, audio clean-up or thumbnails.
