# Short-form script

**This skill adds to your own knowledge; it doesn't limit it.** Answer the user's actual request first and fully: if they asked for a script, the finished script comes first. The rules below are what the studied creators agree on; use your own judgment where they're silent, and say when you do.

## Steps

0. **Match the audience.** Write in the language and register the audience actually speaks (e.g. Hinglish for many Indian audiences; offer both if unsure), and fit the creator's setup (phone, solo, home) *(inferred)*.
1. **Pin the payoff.** Write one sentence: what the viewer knows or can do at the end. Everything else serves it.
2. **Pick a frame.** Prefer "here's what happened when I did it" (a test, a mistake, a result) over "here's what to do". In vidIQ's sample, a creator averaging ≈585 views got 24K with a confession-style title and 138K with "I Tested YouTube Consistency for 90 Days" ([vidIQ, t=0:14–0:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oy0jQZHv2hk&t=14)). If the user has no personal result, use a specific case or a number they can stand behind; never invent a personal story for them.
3. **Write the hook (0–3 s): payoff or proof first.** Three of six creators open with the result or the evidence before any explanation ([Igor, t=0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VCCB_WH0vug&t=0): real analytics screens; [Sky Tan, t=0:00](https://www.instagram.com/p/DambEp1igDW/): the whole structure in 5 s; [vidIQ, t=0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oy0jQZHv2hk&t=0): "We pulled 100 breakout videos"). Rules:
   - Spoken hook ≤ 8 words (≈ 3 s at normal pace), containing a specific number or a concrete noun. If it needs more, put the extra on screen, not in speech.
   - On-screen hook text = 2–4 words, different from the spoken line (it's read in silence; most viewers start muted *(inferred)*). Body beats: 2–5 words of on-screen text each.
   - First frame already shows something: the result, a number, a face mid-gesture. No logo, no "hey guys". If the creator has no real result to show, use the number the video is about (a computed example, labelled "example") — never a fake screenshot presented as theirs.
4. **Lay out the body with open loops.** Default skeleton ([Sky Tan, t=0:00–0:35](https://www.instagram.com/p/DambEp1igDW/)):
   `Hook → Lead (carry them to ~10 s) → Point 1 → open loop → Point 2 → open loop → Point 3 → CTA`
   - Open-loop lines are one sentence that promises the next point without giving it ("That one's common. The second one costs more.").
   - One idea per beat, one sentence per line. A 30–40 s reel holds 3 points at most; with 3 points, shorten the lead to 2–4 s so each point gets ~7 s. For a list topic ("3 mistakes"), the frame from step 2 goes into the hook or the lead ("I made mistake #2 for two years"), not into every point.
5. **Write the call to action.** Ask for one action, tied to the content:
   - Default for accounts without DM automation: the reply-promise CTA below. Use the comment-keyword CTA ("Comment SIP and I'll send the checklist") only when the user can deliver it ([Sky Tan, t=0:31](https://www.instagram.com/p/DambEp1igDW/)). It depends on Instagram DM automation (e.g. ManyChat); status UNVERIFIED for 2026, so check before promising.
   - Reply-promise CTA ("Ask in the comments, I reply to every one"): SpactYT's Short with this CTA drew 32.7 comments per 100 likes, against 2–4 for the other videos studied ([SpactYT, t=1:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uRU2mVSmTyc&t=95)). That's one video; treat it as a strong lead, not a rule.
6. **Check the script against the clock.** At about 2.5 spoken words per second, 30 s ≈ 75 words and 40 s ≈ 100 words *(inferred)*. Cut until it fits; never speed up the delivery to make it fit.
7. **Regulated topics** (money, health, law): add what the topic legally or ethically needs from your own knowledge (e.g. India: mutual-fund risk disclaimer; SEBI rules for finance creators), mark it *(own knowledge, verify current rules)*, and keep any return figure labelled as an assumption.
   - A spoken call to action must never push an irreversible or taxable action (switching funds, selling, stopping a policy) without the caveat in the same breath.
8. **Fact-check every number and claim** in the script before handing it over, and flag anything the user must verify (rates, returns, regulations). One studied reel shipped an impossible example ("1893 Richard Feynman"; Feynman was born in 1918) ([Sky Tan, t=0:20](https://www.instagram.com/p/DambEp1igDW/)).

## After posting (when the user asks what to do next)

- **Outlier loop:** when one video clearly beats their usual numbers, make the same format again with one small twist (most expensive car, then most expensive boat) ([Igor, t=1:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VCCB_WH0vug&t=77)). Post daily while testing formats and lengths ([Igor, t=1:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VCCB_WH0vug&t=71)).
- **Rough YouTube Shorts benchmarks** from one creator's own channel, unverified and date-sensitive: ~40–50% average view duration to get past ~1.5K views, ~60% plus a 3%+ like-to-view ratio for the next tier ([SpactYT, t=0:21–1:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uRU2mVSmTyc&t=21)). YouTube publishes no such tiers.

## Output

1. **Script table**: `Time | Spoken (exact words) | On screen (text / what we see)`, with timings that add up to the target length.
2. **Hook variants**: two alternative hooks (spoken + on-screen), each with a different frame (result-first, number-first or mistake-first).
3. **Titles/captions**: 2 options; for YouTube Shorts, prefer the "I did X" framing from step 2. For Instagram: a first line that repeats the hook, 1–3 lines of value, any required disclaimer, 3–5 specific hashtags *(inferred)*.
4. **Filming notes**: framing, light, audio and delivery for the creator's setup, one line each, plus how many takes per line.
5. **Ready-to-paste caption** for the platform, including any required disclaimer.
6. **Check before filming**: every number or claim the user must verify, plus anything the script assumes about them. Mark anything that came from your own knowledge rather than the sources as *(inferred)*.

## Scope and limits

- Sources are 6 short videos, mostly about YouTube Shorts; Instagram-specific advice comes from one creator. Nothing here covers TikTok-specific features, music licensing or paid promotion.
- Performance numbers (view tiers, retention targets) come from creators' own channels and are unverified.
- Skipped as sources: "10 Proven Video Hooks" (VID HQ: names only, no examples) and Descript's 3 tips (generic).
